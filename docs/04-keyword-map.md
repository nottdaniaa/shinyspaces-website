# ShinySpaces Keyword Map

**Source of truth for this document:** `docs/01-website-blueprint.md` (site structure), `docs/02-business-profile.md` (confirmed business facts — everything else TBD), and `docs/03-seo-strategy.md` (keyword strategy this map operationalizes).

**Guardrails applied throughout:**
- No search volume or keyword difficulty numbers are stated anywhere — none were measured with a keyword-research tool. Every keyword below is a **recommendation**, not a measured metric.
- No ranking is claimed or implied for any keyword.
- One **primary** keyword is assigned per page specifically to prevent keyword cannibalization (two pages competing for the same search term). Where two pages are topically close (e.g., `/contact` and `/get-estimate`), their primary keywords are deliberately differentiated — see those entries and the Keyword Coverage Review at the end.
- **Assumptions** are called out explicitly in each entry where this document makes a judgment call not directly stated in the source documents (e.g., which towns most need Airbnb-specific emphasis). These are directional opinions, not confirmed facts, and should be validated before being treated as final.
- No website body copy is written here — only SEO targets, structural metadata, and planning notes.

# Keyword Coverage Review

## Keyword Gaps

- **No page currently targets purely informational, top-of-funnel research queries** (e.g., "how much does house cleaning cost," "how often should I clean my Airbnb between guests"). These are addressed only as *future* blog topics in `03-seo-strategy.md` §13 — until that content exists, the site has no page competing for informational search volume, which may limit total organic reach even if lead-gen pages perform well.
- **No dedicated page targets property managers or real estate agents specifically**, even though both are confirmed target customers (`02-business-profile.md` §5). They're currently folded into the Commercial Cleaning and Recurring Cleaning pages' audience notes rather than having distinct language. Consider whether a short dedicated section (not necessarily a full new page) addressing property managers/real estate agents directly is worth adding to the Commercial and Recurring pages.
- **No page addresses pricing directly**, since pricing structure is TBD (`02-business-profile.md` §3). Once pricing philosophy is confirmed, a pricing-related FAQ entry or dedicated section should be added — pricing-related queries ("how much does house cleaning cost in Heber City") are a common and currently unaddressed search pattern.
- **Trust/credential-specific queries** (e.g., "insured house cleaner Heber City," "background checked cleaning service Utah") currently have no page that can honestly target them, since licensing/insurance/background-check status is unconfirmed (`02-business-profile.md` §9). This is a gap that can only be closed with real information, not copy changes.

## Pages Competing for the Same Keyword

- **Homepage vs. Services Hub:** both were initially assigned "cleaning services Heber City" as the primary keyword (per `03-seo-strategy.md` §7–8). This is a direct cannibalization risk. **Resolution applied in this document:** the Services Hub's primary keyword has been changed to a more comparison/list-oriented term ("types of cleaning services offered"), leaving "cleaning services Heber City" as the homepage's sole primary keyword. Recommend updating `03-seo-strategy.md` to reflect this same adjustment for consistency.
- **`/contact` vs. `/get-estimate`:** both serve the same ultimate action (getting in touch) and could easily end up targeting the same phrase if not deliberately separated. This document assigns "contact ShinySpaces cleaning" to `/contact` (informational/navigational) and "free cleaning estimate Heber City" to `/get-estimate` (transactional/bottom-of-funnel) — these are differentiated by intent, not just wording, which should hold up better than a superficial synonym swap.
- **Individual town pages vs. each other:** all 8 pages share the same H1/title pattern ("Cleaning Services in [Town], UT"), which is by design for local SEO, but the *secondary* keyword and content emphasis must stay genuinely differentiated (see the town table above) — otherwise these 8 pages risk cannibalizing each other for the shared base phrase "cleaning services [town] UT" pattern, and Google may also flag them as thin/duplicate content if body copy isn't meaningfully varied (already flagged in the blueprint, §4 note, and in `03-seo-strategy.md` §10).

## Opportunities to Consolidate or Expand

- **Consolidate:** The mini-FAQ sections planned on each individual service page (blueprint §4) and the full `/faq` page both target overlapping question territory. Recommend keeping service-page FAQs strictly limited to 2–3 questions *specific to that service* (as already scoped in the blueprint) and reserving broader/cross-cutting questions (pricing philosophy, service area, trust/safety) exclusively for the `/faq` page — this avoids the two content areas duplicating the same Q&A text sitewide.
- **Expand:** Once pricing philosophy and trust signals (licensing, insurance, etc.) are confirmed, expand the FAQ page with a dedicated "Pricing" and "Trust & Safety" subsection with real, specific answers rather than the current placeholder-level grouping — this is likely to capture meaningful informational search volume that no page currently addresses (see Keyword Gaps above).
- **Expand:** Consider whether property managers and real estate agents warrant their own short dedicated page (e.g., `/services/property-management-cleaning`) once real demand data exists — for now, folding them into Commercial and Recurring Cleaning avoids fragmenting content prematurely, but this should be revisited if that segment turns out to be a larger lead source than expected.
- **Consolidate (technical):** Because the 6 service pages and 8 town pages each follow a near-identical template (per blueprint §7), a shared content structure (e.g., a small data file per service/town feeding one dynamic route) is worth implementing at the code level once all copy is finalized — this only affects implementation, not the keyword targets themselves, and involves no change to any current document.
