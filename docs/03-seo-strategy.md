# ShinySpaces SEO Strategy

**Source of truth for this document:** `docs/01-website-blueprint.md` (site structure/pages) and `docs/02-business-profile.md` (confirmed facts — all other business details remain TBD).

**A note on keywords throughout this document:** No search volume, keyword difficulty, or ranking-probability figures are stated anywhere below. Actual search volume and difficulty require a keyword-research tool (Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush, etc.) connected to real data — this document was not produced with access to one. Every keyword listed is a **recommendation** based on the business's services, service area, and customer intent, not a measured metric. Before finalizing a keyword list, run these terms through a keyword tool to validate real volume/difficulty for the Heber Valley/Utah market.

---

# 1. SEO Goals

**Primary business objective**
Per `02-business-profile.md` §8, the website's primary goal is lead generation — converting visitors into **estimate requests** and **phone calls**. SEO exists to serve this goal, not vanity traffic. Every recommendation in this document is judged by whether it's likely to bring in people actively looking to book a cleaning, not just general visitors.

**Organic traffic goals (directional, not measured)**
- Recommended focus: rank for service + location combinations where local intent is highest (e.g., "Airbnb cleaning Park City," "house cleaning Heber City") rather than chasing broad national terms like "cleaning services," which are dominated by large national brands and franchises and carry lower buyer intent for a locally-bound business.
- Recommended early milestone: visibility in Google's local map pack for the core service area, which typically matters more for a local service business than blog-driven organic traffic volume.
- No specific traffic numbers (e.g., "X visits/month") are set here — those should be defined by the business owner once real baseline data exists (Google Search Console / Analytics), per `02-business-profile.md` §8 "Success metrics / KPIs — TBD."

**Lead generation goals**
- Every primary page (service pages, town pages, homepage) should have one clear conversion path: the estimate form or click-to-call, per the CTA components already defined in the blueprint (§3 Global Components: Estimate Request Form, CTA Banner, Sticky Mobile CTA Bar).
- Recommended approach: treat `/get-estimate` as the primary SEO/PPC landing page for high-intent queries, keeping it lean per the blueprint (§4), since simpler landing pages tend to convert better for direct-response goals.

# 2. Target Audience: Search Intent by Customer Type

### Homeowners
- **Intent:** Mostly transactional/commercial ("near me" and city-name searches) mixed with some early research intent ("how much does house cleaning cost," "how often should I clean my house").
- **Example query patterns:** "house cleaning Heber City," "residential cleaning near me," "weekly house cleaning service Midway."
- **What they need to see:** Clear service description, service area confirmation, an easy way to request a quote, and trust signals (once real ones exist — see `02-business-profile.md` §9).

### Airbnb Hosts / Vacation Rental Owners
- **Intent:** Highly transactional and urgency-driven — often searching same-day or scheduling-adjacent terms.
- **Example query patterns:** "Airbnb cleaning service Park City," "same day turnover cleaning Heber Valley," "vacation rental cleaning near me," "STR turnover cleaner Utah."
- **What they need to see:** Confirmation the business understands turnover timing/guest schedules (per blueprint's Airbnb page copy), scheduling flexibility, and a fast way to inquire — this audience converts on speed and reliability signals.

### Property Managers
- **Intent:** Transactional but often B2B in tone — may be researching a vendor for multiple properties rather than a single job.
- **Example query patterns:** "cleaning company for rental properties Heber City," "recurring cleaning contractor Park City," "property management cleaning vendor Utah."
- **What they need to see:** Evidence of reliability at scale (recurring cleaning capability, multiple towns served), and a clear point of contact for ongoing/contract-style work rather than a single one-off booking.

### Commercial Businesses
- **Intent:** Transactional, often researching recurring contracts rather than one-time service.
- **Example query patterns:** "commercial cleaning Heber City," "office cleaning service near me," "small business cleaning contractor Utah."
- **What they need to see:** Confirmation the business handles commercial (not just residential) spaces, flexible scheduling around business hours (per blueprint's Commercial page copy), and a straightforward way to request a quote.

# 9. Internal Linking Strategy

- **Hub-and-spoke model:** `/services` is the hub linking out to all 6 service pages; `/service-areas` is the hub linking out to all 8 town pages. Every service page links back to its hub and forward to related town pages; every town page links back to its hub and forward to the services most relevant to that town.
- **Cross-linking services ↔ towns:** e.g., the Airbnb Turnover Cleaning page should link to Park City and Midway (higher rental density per Section 6's directional note); the Commercial Cleaning page should link to Heber City (denser business core).
- **Footer as a sitewide link layer:** per blueprint §3, the footer already lists all services and all towns — this gives every page on the site a path to every other core page, which is good for both SEO crawlability and user navigation.
- **Anchor text guidance:** use descriptive, keyword-relevant anchor text ("Airbnb turnover cleaning in Park City" rather than "click here" or bare "Park City").
- **Breadcrumbs:** recommended on service and town pages (e.g., Home > Services > Airbnb Turnover Cleaning) to reinforce hierarchy for both users and search engines — see Structured Data section for BreadcrumbList schema.

# 10. Local SEO Strategy

**Google Business Profile**
- Create/claim a Google Business Profile once real business details (address or defined service area, phone, hours — currently TBD per `02-business-profile.md` §2) are finalized.
- Category recommendation: primary category "House Cleaning Service," with secondary categories for "Commercial Cleaning Service" if available, to reflect the full service mix.
- List the specific service area towns in the profile's service-area settings rather than a single pin, since ShinySpaces serves multiple towns (per business profile §4).
- Do not publish the profile with placeholder hours/phone/address — wait for confirmed data.

**NAP (Name, Address, Phone) Consistency**
- Name, address/service-area statement, and phone number must match exactly across the website footer, Contact page, Google Business Profile, and any future directory listings.
- **Current blocker:** phone number, address, and hours are all marked TBD in `02-business-profile.md` §2 — no NAP citations should be published anywhere (site or directories) until these are confirmed, to avoid having to correct inconsistent listings later, which can hurt local rankings.

**Service Area Pages**
- Follow the blueprint's existing guidance (§4) to keep each town page substantive and non-duplicate — vary the intro paragraph and reference locally-relevant use cases per town (e.g., short-term rental focus for Park City, residential/rural focus for Charleston and Wallsburg) rather than templating identical text with only the town name swapped.
- Each town page should have its own title/meta/H1 (Section 8) — never reuse the exact same metadata across town pages.

**Reviews**
- Recommend a simple post-service review request process (e.g., a follow-up text/email with a direct link to the Google Business Profile review form) once that profile exists.
- Do not display a star rating or review count anywhere on the site until there is a real, verifiable number to show — see `02-business-profile.md` §9 and §11.
- Once real reviews exist, feature a rotating selection on the homepage and full-page grid on `/reviews`, per the blueprint's existing Testimonial Carousel component.

**Photos**
- Real photos matter significantly for local SEO (Google Business Profile photo engagement) and for on-site trust — per blueprint §5 and business profile §10, real before/after and team photos are still needed.
- Recommend geotagging/naming image files descriptively (e.g., `airbnb-cleaning-park-city-before-after.jpg`) and adding descriptive alt text for accessibility and image search visibility.

# 11. Structured Data (Schema.org Recommendations)

All schema below should only be populated with **confirmed** data. Fields still TBD per `02-business-profile.md` must be omitted rather than filled with placeholders.

- **LocalBusiness** (or a more specific subtype if one fits better once confirmed): `name` (ShinySpaces), `areaServed` (the 8 towns), `address`/`telephone`/`openingHours` — **all currently TBD, omit until confirmed**.
- **Service** (used per service page to represent "CleaningService"-style offerings): `serviceType` (e.g., "Airbnb Turnover Cleaning"), `provider` (linked to the LocalBusiness/Organization entity), `areaServed`.
- **FAQPage:** apply to the `/faq` page (and optionally the mini-FAQ sections on service pages per blueprint §4), marking up each question/answer pair.
- **BreadcrumbList:** apply to service pages and town pages reflecting the hierarchy described in Section 9 (Home > Services > [Service] / Home > Service Areas > [Town]).
- **WebSite:** basic sitewide markup with `name` and `url`; a `SearchAction` can be added later if on-site search is ever built.
- **Organization:** `name`, `url`, `logo` (once a logo file exists — currently TBD per business profile §1), and `sameAs` links to social profiles (currently TBD per business profile §2).

**Do not include** `aggregateRating` or `review` schema until a real, verifiable rating/review count exists — adding this prematurely or with placeholder values would violate Google's structured data guidelines and the constraint in `02-business-profile.md` §11.

# 14. AI Search Optimization

As AI assistants and AI-powered search features (e.g., AI Overviews, chatbot-style answer engines) increasingly summarize and recommend local businesses directly, the site should be written to be easy for these systems to extract and trust:

- **State facts plainly and consistently.** Repeat the same core facts — business name, services offered, towns served — in the same wording across the homepage, About page, and footer. Inconsistent phrasing (e.g., listing different towns in different places) makes it harder for AI systems to confidently extract a single accurate answer.
- **Avoid vague marketing language where a concrete fact would do.** "We serve homeowners, Airbnb hosts, property managers, and small businesses across Heber City, Midway, Park City, Kamas, Hideout, Daniel, Charleston, and Wallsburg" is easier for an AI system to parse and repeat than "we proudly serve the whole valley."
- **Use structured data as a machine-readable backup** to the visible text (Section 11) — this gives AI systems and search engines a second, unambiguous source for the same facts stated in the copy.
- **FAQ-style content is especially valuable for AI extraction** — direct question/answer pairs (Section 13's FAQ page, and mini-FAQs on service pages per the blueprint) map naturally onto how AI answer engines look for citable, quotable answers.
- **Never let on-site claims outpace confirmed facts.** Per `02-business-profile.md` §11, unverified claims (years in business, certifications, review counts, guarantees) must not appear anywhere — this matters doubly for AI search, since an AI system repeating a fabricated claim to a user is a direct business/trust risk once it's ever fact-checked or contradicted elsewhere (e.g., on the Google Business Profile).
- **Keep service descriptions specific rather than generic.** "Airbnb Turnover Cleaning" pages should clearly state what's included (per the blueprint's existing "What's Included" checklist sections) so an AI system can accurately answer a user's specific question ("does this company restock guest supplies?") rather than giving a vague non-answer.
